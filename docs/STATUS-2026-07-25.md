### The finding worth underlining
The restore drill restored 107,587 rows in 12.6s. Row counts matched. `board_frames` came back **27,964 of 27,964 days, zero gaps**.

**And every frame was corrupt.** `$n::bytea` on PostgREST's hex string stores literal ASCII instead of running `bytea_in`, double-encoding every blob.

Row count and contiguity are the exact two checks Ruling 5 named — **both passed on 100% corrupt data.** Only a canonical per-row digest computed in SQL and compared byte-for-byte against live production caught it (`f8d73fae…`, matching on all 27,964; a third path via `pg_dump -Fc` agrees).

An untested backup is a belief — and the verification criteria we wrote were themselves insufficient. A table whose payload is a packed blob cannot be verified by counting rows.

The drill also found that `hunt_seasons`/`hunt_zones` reference `hunt_species` and `hunt_states`, neither of which was in the backup set: **the countdown's season data was unrestorable.**

## 4. DECISIONS NEEDED

### 4.1 Season dates — scope
**Recommendation: openers only.** The countdown needs **one date per state per species** — ~100 numbers. We captured 357 records with 269 date spans, zone structures, per-segment bag limits and closes. Nearly all the cost and nearly all the error risk lives in structure the countdown never reads.

Everything that went wrong was structural, never the opener: North Dakota's unsigned proclamation, New York's map contradicting its own table, per-segment limits, Sunday-closure states where 69 calendar days are a legal 60-day season. The openers themselves came back **CLEAN** on an independent eight-pair re-transcription.

`hunt_regulation_links` already holds 243 official state URLs. So: transcribe openers, link out for zones and bag limits. That is *more* trustworthy, not less — we stop pretending to be a regulations source and point at the authority, which is where a hunter should read bag limits anyway, because being wrong there is a citation.

Nothing captured is lost. What narrows is the **maintenance commitment**.

**The asymmetry is the real argument:** the card and the map need *zero* annual maintenance — 1950–2025 does not change. Season dates are the only part of this product that decays on a timer.

### 4.2 ERA5 faucet — needs your hands
Both fast paths are blocked on things I will not do: Copernicus CDS needs an **account**, Open-Meteo Professional needs a **card**.

| path | time | cost | note |
|---|---|---|---|

| Open-Meteo Professional | ~3.3 h | one month | historical requires Professional; Standard excluded |
| **Copernicus CDS** | ~1 day + queue | $0 | **recommended** — kills the quota *and* the non-commercial licence permanently |

### 4.3 Backup destination — needs one credential
Ruling 5 condition 1 is **not satisfied**: the dump is outside the cluster but still on one Mac — a second copy, not a second failure domain. `DCD_BACKUP_SYNC_CMD` is wired and fires only after every table verifies.

Two recommendations: a vendor **outside AWS us-west-2**, so one regional event cannot take the cluster and the backup together; and schedule **after 11:45 UTC**, because backups land ~08:40 and the bake writes at 11:45 — today's frame is never in today's backup.

### 4.4 Do not narrate the decade trend
Amendment 1.2 §4 made the trend a headline sentence: *"these are getting rarer."* On real data that is not supportable — the **direction flips with the band**:

| Maryland, Oct 10 | 1980s | 1990s | 2000s | 2010s | 2020s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| coldest 5% | 0.80 | 0.50 | 0.60 | 0.50 | 0.33* |
| coldest 2% | 0.10 | 0.20 | 0.20 | 0.30 | 0.33* |

Same state, same day, same record — declining at 5%, rising monotonically at 2%. At n≈12–27 the decade shape is noise-dominated, and the band is a parameter we have deliberately not fixed.

**Recommendation: draw the bars, never state the direction.** The bars are census and honest. A sentence claiming direction is an inference that reverses under a different band — exactly the kind of confident claim that killed gates 1 and 2.

## 6. PROCESS CHANGE

**No more than two committing agents at once.** Two integrity incidents came out of one four-agent fan-out:

- an agent ran `git commit --amend` after another had committed, rewriting **that agent's** commit and folding an 8,000-line diff into a 9-line fix
- another ran `reset --hard` on the shared tree twice, wiping a third agent's **uncommitted** front-door work

Both recovered and verified. Both are my fault: one-write-pipe discipline was applied rigorously to the database and never to git. Standing rules now: agents commit forward only — never amend, rebase, reset or force-push — stage explicit paths, and never push. The main session pushes after verifying each reported SHA is reachable and that no commit absorbed another's files.

`--amend` destroys history and the reflog can save you. `reset --hard` destroys work that was never committed and leaves no trace of what was lost.
